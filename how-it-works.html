How It Works: Three Processes, Before and After

The fastest way to see what "eliminating manual processes" means is to watch the same three documents twice — once through the manual steps they travel today, and once with those steps removed. Same documents, same decisions, same people where judgement is needed. Different amount of errand work in between.

Process 1

The invoice that was re-keyed by hand

Before: a supplier invoice arrives as a PDF attachment. Someone opens it, reads the vendor, number, line items, and total, and types all of it into the accounting system. A second person spot-checks the entry — or doesn't. Days later, a mismatch between the invoice and the purchase order surfaces at payment time, and the detective work begins in inboxes and folders.

After: the invoice is captured the moment it arrives. MaxxOwl™ SI reads it once and extracts the same fields as structured data. The data is validated and matched against the purchase order and goods receipt with N-Way Match™; if a price or quantity disagrees, the invoice becomes an exception in Outcome Workspace™, with the document and the discrepancy side by side for a person to decide. If it matches, it moves to approval as a task — and the finished record, match and all, is written to the Outcome Repository™. Nobody typed the invoice. Somebody decided the exception. That is the division of labour the process always wanted.

Process 2

The form that was chased by email

Before: a request form goes out by email. It comes back half completed — or as a photograph of a printout, or not at all. The follow-up is a thread of reminders, each one a small interruption, and the work cannot start because two required items are still missing and nobody can say exactly which two.

After: the form lives in a portal — MaxxLaunch™, the forms and portals intake on the MaxxLogix platform. The submission is checked for completeness as it is made: required fields, required documents, valid formats. What arrives is a complete packet of structured data, not a thread to untangle, and it enters the same workflow as any document — checked, routed, worked, and recorded. Follow-up stops being an email campaign and becomes a status the requester can see. The reminders that remain are the platform's, issued by the workflow on schedule — not drafted from memory by whoever happens to remember the request is waiting.

Process 3

The contract that was printed to sign

Before: the contract is agreed, then printed. It is signed in ink, scanned, emailed back as a grainy attachment, and saved — somewhere. Which copy is the executed one? Where is the evidence of who signed, and when? Those questions get answered later, slowly, by whoever kept the best inbox.

After: the contract is signed in the flow — no print, no scan, no re-attach. The moment the signature is complete, the signed contract is filed in the Outcome Repository™ together with its history: who signed, when, and what the document looked like at signing. The executed copy is the only copy anyone has to find, and finding it is a search. The paper detour is simply gone from the middle of a digital process — along with the filing question it used to leave behind. (The signing step itself is covered in depth at SecureSignNow.)

The pattern underneath all three

Read the three "after" versions again and the same shape appears: the document is read once, its data moves on its own, exceptions surface to a person with full context, and the outcome is recorded once. The manual steps were never three different problems. They were one problem — document work organised around people fetching, carrying, and re-typing — wearing three different outfits. Remove the carrying, and what remains is the work itself: decisions, approvals, and answers.
