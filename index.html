Eliminate the Manual Steps Hiding Inside Document Work

Every document process hides people doing a machine's errands: retyping data from a PDF into another system, emailing to ask if anyone has approved it yet, printing a contract so it can be signed and scanned back in. Eliminating manual processes means handing those steps to a platform built for them — so people's hours go to judgement, and every document ends as an outcome: what got done with it.

The manual steps nobody budgets for

Manual steps rarely appear on a process chart, but they are where the time goes. A document arrives; someone opens it to find out what it is. Its data gets keyed into a system by hand, then keyed again somewhere else. An approval depends on an email somebody may or may not have seen. When a question comes up weeks later, someone hunts through folders and inboxes for the current version — and for proof of who signed off.

None of these steps require expertise — just availability and patience — and they interrupt the work that does need a person's judgement. They also introduce the quiet risks of hand work: a transposed digit, a version that was not final, a filing step that never happened. The process looks staffed; in truth it is held together by errands.

Five manual steps, and what replaces each one

Step 1

Re-keying data from PDFs

The manual way: open the PDF, read it, type its contents into the system. The replacement: MaxxOwl™ SI reads each document once — classifying it and extracting its data as structured fields — and the data moves on its own. Typing becomes reviewing, and only where the read needs a second look.

Step 2

Chasing approvals by email

The manual way: forward, wait, nudge, repeat — while the document's status lives in someone's memory. The replacement: approvals become tasks in a workflow, routed by rule and tracked in Outcome Workspace™. Status is something you read, not something you chase.

Step 3

Printing a document to sign it

The manual way: print, sign, scan, re-attach — a paper detour in the middle of digital work. The replacement: the signature happens inside the flow, and the signed record is filed with its history on completion.

Step 4

Hunting for the current version

The manual way: three copies in three places, and a debate about which one is real. The replacement: one authoritative record in the Outcome Repository™, every version and change accounted for. Finding it becomes a search, not an investigation.

Step 5

Re-filing what was already filed

The manual way: download it, rename it, upload it somewhere else — the same document handled again because the systems never met. The replacement: the document is captured once, and the outcome is recorded once. Filing is where the process ends, not a second job after it.

What people do with the time back

Eliminating manual steps is not about removing people from document work. It is about which parts of the work people touch. When reading, checking, routing, and filing run themselves, the human moments are the ones that were always supposed to be human: the exception that needs a decision, the approval that needs a judgement call, the person who needs an answer.

That is the honest trade. The platform takes the errands; the team takes the exceptions. Work stops being measured in documents touched and starts being measured in outcomes delivered.

Frequently asked questions

Which manual steps disappear first?

Usually the ones hiding in plain sight: re-keying data out of PDFs and email attachments, chasing approvals through inboxes, and re-filing documents into a second system. Those steps need no judgement, so they are the safest to hand to the platform — and their absence is felt immediately, because the chasing stops.

Does eliminating manual steps mean eliminating people?

No — it means eliminating errands. The platform reads, checks, routes, and files; people decide. Exceptions and approvals still land with a person, with the document and its data in front of them. The job changes from moving paper to making the calls the paper was waiting on.

What happens when the platform cannot read a document?

It says so. Low-confidence reads and failed checks become exceptions, routed to a person with the document, the extracted data, and the reason it was flagged. Nothing is guessed at silently, and nothing stalls in an inbox unnoticed.

How is this different from just digitising the paper?

A scanned document can still demand every manual step that followed the paper one — someone opens it, reads it, retypes it, and files it. Digitising changes the format; eliminating manual processes changes the work. The document is read once, its data moves on its own, and the finish is an outcome on the record.

How is capacity handled as volume grows?

Capacity is published in SI Units, so you can see exactly what a plan includes before you commit. When your workload grows, you can add capacity — that decision is always yours. There are no surprise overage bills for work the platform already processed.
